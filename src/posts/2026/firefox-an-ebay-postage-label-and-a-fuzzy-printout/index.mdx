I recently sold something on eBay UK. The buyer was in the Channel Islands. And
apparently that means you need to affix a customs declaration to the parcel.
Handily, eBay provide a customs declaration label for you. And so I wanted to
print that out (along with the postage label).[^1]

Until recently, I had been using the free version of Adobe Acrobat to do this.
That was simply as I trusted it to print labels and barcodes properly (which
includes being able to print at the original scale – that is without scaling the
document to fit within the printer margins).

But Adobe Acrobat was increasingly becoming unbearable to use. (I’m sure many
would say it’s been like that for aeons, but I’d mostly put up with it until
now.) That includes terrible scrolling, generally being slow, and random panels
popping up or reappearing and getting in my way. And to top it off, commenter
Andrew B mentioned on the post
[The Windows 11 installation that was eating my disk space](/2025/12/06/the-windows-11-installation-that-was-eating-my-disk-space/)
that Adobe Acrobat has a habit of storing tens of gigabytes of Windows Installer
patches in the `C:\Windows\Installer` directory. (A fact that I had been
oblivious to until then.)

Now, I generally use Firefox to browse the web. It, of course, has a built-in
PDF viewer and it can also print without scaling. So, I thought I’d use it on
this occasion to print the labels for my parcel going to the Channel Islands.

Alas, that proved to be a bad idea, as the customs declaration that came out of
my printer was unreadable in parts. Here is a crop of a 1600dpi scan of what it

To the naked eye, those coloured areas just look grey. My assumption was that
some processing in Firefox has introduced grey everywhere, and my inkjet printer
is using colour ink to represent grey (or at least the shades of grey used).

I thought about how I could check if that was indeed the case. Printing to a
file of some kind seemed like the most obvious way. And my rudimentary way of
doing that is to use the ‘Microsoft Print to PDF’ virtual printer.[^3]

It indeed turns out that printing to that virtual printer from Firefox adds grey
to the QR code that isn’t there if you do the same thing from Adobe Acrobat.

I should mention that the complete QR code still scans fine, so the slight loss
of fidelity in it probably isn’t such a big deal. And while the labels for my
Channel Islands parcel were just two images inserted into a PDF, normal Royal
Mail postage labels (without a customs declaration) provided by eBay are much
nicer and contain proper text (only using images for barcodes and logos). Those
normal labels come out much better when printed using Firefox.

I had a nose around the Mozilla Bugzilla and came across these potentially
relevant bugs:

- [1774615 - Avoid some printing blurriness because of rasterized group.](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1774615)
- [1863302 - The PDF printing quality is low](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1863302)
- [1269023 - poor print quality from pdf on 332-2012.pdf](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1269023)
- [852536 - low quality bar code pdf](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=852536)

None of those stand out as being about exactly the same thing at first sight.
Looking closer, though,
[comment 11 on bug 1863302](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1863302#c11)
sounds very similar:

> FYI I just run into this issue while printing a shipping document. I've
> printed it from the Firefox PDF viewer and the barcode, text and QR code
> looked rather fuzzy. I opened the same document in Edge and printed it out
> using the exact same settings and got a crisp print.

During my own rudimentary investigation with the ‘Microsoft Print to PDF’
printer driver and a few postage labels, I noticed that Firefox seems to be
resampling the images in the PDFs during printing by factors like 0.5x or 2x.
Acrobat, on the other hand, was preserving the original resolution of the
images. And so my suspicion is that the cause of the problem is Firefox deciding
to resample the images in the PDFs during printing, though there could easily be
more going on that I’m not aware of.

After all that, I hadn’t solved my problem of ditching Acrobat. I had a look at
some alternatives and tested out how they fared at printing.

I tried printing the customs declaration label with Microsoft Edge, the free
version of [Foxit PDF Reader](https://www.foxit.com/pdf-reader/), and the free
version of [PDF-XChange Editor](https://www.pdf-xchange.com). They all printed
it fine. I also had a quick look at
[Sumatra PDF](https://www.sumatrapdfreader.org/free-pdf-reader) and
[Okular](https://okular.kde.org/en-gb/). While both were able to print PDFs,
they were lacking in printing options (not even having their own printing
dialogue). I knew that neither of them would be suitable for me, and so I didn’t
bother doing a test print with them.

As it happened, I also had a large diagram to print out. I printed it with
Acrobat, Foxit PDF Reader and PDF-XChange Editor as another test. To my
surprise, Acrobat failed here. The diagram had some boxes shaded with diagonal
lines going bottom-left to top-right, and some other boxes shaded with diagonal
lines going from top-left to bottom-right. While that shading looked fine on
screen (and in the print preview), for some reason, the lines going bottom-left
to top-right were missing in the actual printed version (while the ones going
from top-left to bottom-right came out fine). Using my new-found trick of
investigating printing problems by printing to a PDF, I found Acrobat had made
the missing lines very thin in the printout (seemingly so thin that I couldn’t
see them on paper).

Foxit PDF Reader and PDF-XChange Editor both printed the diagram correctly, with
both types of diagonal-line-shading intact. Comparing all three printouts, there
were some slight colour differences for whatever reason. I’m not trying to print
colour-accurate PDFs (at least at the moment), so I’m not so bothered about
that. I did also notice that some very fine coloured lines were a bit thicker or
more saturated in the Foxit printout.

Putting any slight colour differences aside, I have some working options for
printing PDFs that aren’t Adobe Acrobat. Currently, PDF-XChange Editor is my
favourite, simply because I prefer its print dialogue. That dialogue is
resizeable and has a large print preview, which shows the printer margins and
makes it clear if anything will be cut off when printing at 100% scale. (I
wasn’t as much of a fan of their company’s website design, though.)

I recently visited Japan for a third time. Having previously stayed in Tokyo and
Kyoto, I was keen to explore some new areas on this trip (and see some autumn
colours, as I had done in 2017).

([I previously posted about my second trip](/2024/07/04/a-trip-to-japan/),
though there is no post for the first visit.)

## Nikkō

My first stop was Nikkō. Mountains provided a majestic backdrop with low clouds
making a frequent appearance.

<CaptionedImage
  src={nikko_road_mountains_avif}
  alt="A road near the city centre in Nikkō, with mountains visible in the background."
  caption="Mountains being just there felt a bit surreal"
/>

<CaptionedImage
  src={nikko_cafe_avif}
  alt="A café against a backdrop of low clouds."
  caption="Clouds loomed low behind this Strawberry Village Café branch just after sunset"
/>

My first sightseeing stop was Kirifuri Falls (霧降の滝, Kirifuri no taki). It
was very windy on the exposed observation deck on the day I visited. And there
was spray being blown about, though I couldn’t quite work out where it was
coming from. (I seemed too far away from the waterfall for that to be the
source. It felt like drizzle being blown by the wind, but it seemed to persist
when there was no cloud overhead.)

While the conditions may have felt unpleasant, I was rewarded with a spectacular
view, complete with a rainbow.

<CaptionedImage
  src={kirifuri_no_taki_avif}
  alt="A wide view of Kirifuri Falls and the surrounding foliage, with a small rainbow at the top of the scene."
  caption="The rainbow was a nice surprise"
/>

There were some autumn colours developing (I imagine it would’ve looked even
better a week later).

There were other people visiting the observation deck at the same time as me.
But not many.

After Kirifuri Falls, I passed by Shinkyō Bridge (神橋, Shinkyō).

<CaptionedImage
  src={shinkyo_bridge_avif}
  alt="A view of Shinkyō Bridge and the Daiya River."
  caption="A popular tourist spot"
/>

That was also a pretty sight, though busier (and difficult to frame well in a
photo).

(I used the rest of that day to explore the shrines in the World Heritage Site
and the Tamozawa Imperial Villa. I don’t really have anything to share about
those, so I’ll skip over them.)

On my second full day in Nikkō, I travelled uphill along the second Irohazaka
winding road to visit the area near Lake Chūzenji (中禅寺湖, Chūzenji-ko) in
Oku-Nikkō.

My first stop was a busy Kegon Falls (華厳の滝, Kegon no taki).

<CaptionedImage
  src={kegon_falls_avif}
  alt="A wide view of Kegon Falls."
  caption="The view from the lower observation decks, accessed via a lift"
/>

Supposedly this was the peak period for autumn colours at the waterfall. Peak or
not, the leaves looked simultaneously dead and green to me.

I next took the bus to the Mount Hangetsu (半月山, Hangetsuyama or Hangetsusan)
car park and followed the trail up to the observation deck near the summit of
the mountain. It was a moderately difficult climb that took me 20 minutes
(lugging along my camera gear at the same time). That effort was rewarded with
stunning sweeping views of Lake Chūzenji.

<CaptionedImage
  src={hangetsuyama_view_avif}
  alt="A view of Lake Chūzenji from the Hangetsuyama observation deck."
  caption="A lake and mountains, what more do you need?"
/>

Cloud blocked part of the view, but it was spectacular nonetheless. There were
probably about seven or eight people at a time on the observation deck, with
some choosing to eat lunch or a snack there (with hindsight I should’ve brought
something to eat too).

I then retreated to the car park to wait for the bus back to Lake Chūzenji. The
car park acted as an observation deck in itself, and had good views of other
nearby mountains (but not of Lake Chūzenji).

<CaptionedImage
  src={hangetsuyama_car_park_mountains_avif}
  alt="A view from the Hangetsuyama car park towards nearby mountains."
  caption="The south-east view from the car park"
/>

While I was waiting for the bus, cloud seemed to move in near the path up the
mountain.

<CaptionedImage
  src={hangetsuyama_car_park_cloud_avif}
  alt="A view from the Hangetsuyama car park towards the path up the mountain with cloud obscuring part of the mountain."
  caption="I probably came back down at the right time"
/>

## Naruko Gorge

After Nikkō, I travelled to Sendai. I picked Sendai as it was a convenient place
from which to visit some other sights that I wanted to see.

The first of those was Naruko Gorge (鳴子峡, Narukokyō). My timing worked out
well as I visited when autumn colours were at their peak.

I took many photos of Ofukazawa Bridge and the surrounding foliage, but my
favourite was when there was a bit of shade. That shade resulted in the
different colours being evenly lit.

<CaptionedImage
  src={naruko_gorge_bridge_avif}
  alt="A view of Ofukazawa Bridge and the surrounding autumn foliage."
  caption="Ofukazawa Bridge, as seen from near Narukokyō Rest House"
/>

The view further to the right was also pretty.

<CaptionedImage
  src={naruko_gorge_koyo_avif}
  alt="A view of autumn foliage near Naruko Gorge."
  caption="More pretty foliage viewed from the same location"
/>

I descended into the gorge via the western walking trail, which also had some
pleasant views. This was the view at the end of the path.

<CaptionedImage
  src={naruko_gorge_west_trail_avif}
  alt="A portrait view of Naruko Gorge from the end of the west walking trail."
  caption="The path beyond this point has been closed since 2008 after an earthquake"
  class="limit-height-slightly"
/>

On the bus back towards Naruko Onsen station, I got off at the stop for the
eastern walking trail. I wasn’t sure whether visiting it was worth the effort,
but I had some time, and so I thought I may as well.

It was deserted. Some ominous traffic cones were at the entrance to the trail.
The gate to the trail was open, but the path was wet and muddy. I walked down
the path, and, quite frankly, there wasn’t much to see.

A photo I took came out reasonably well.

<CaptionedImage
  src={naruko_gorge_east_trail_avif}
  alt="A view of the eastern Naruko Gorge walking trail."
  caption="The eastern walking trail was very short"
/>

But, honestly, that photo is overselling it. The abruptly ending trail really
wasn’t worth the effort.

From there, I walked back towards my arrival point, Naruko Onsen station. As I’d
got off the bus early, I’d missed the previous train back towards Sendai and had
to wait a while for the next one. It wasn’t a dull wait though, as I heard some
music and walked towards it, ending up at a mini Halloween event with some live
music. (Perhaps aimed at a younger demographic, mind you.)

## Yamadera

The other excursion I wanted to make from Sendai was to the Risshaku-ji temple,
also known as Yamadera. The temple is set on a mountainside and has around 1000
steps. Near the top is a small observation deck called Godaidō Hall. That
observation deck was crowded, but had awesome (in the traditional sense) views.

<CaptionedImage
  src={yamadera_view_avif}
  alt="A view of a valley and surrounding mountains from Godaidō Hall at the top of Yamadera temple."
  caption="I heard a lot of きれいだね (beautiful, isn’t it?) while up there"
/>

While it was a bit of effort to get up there, I didn’t find it as difficult as
1000 steps might sound.

## Ōsaka

I’d never been to Ōsaka before, so I made that my next stop. It had a different
feel to the other locations, as, well, it was full of (other) tourists.

Still, Ōsaka Castle (大阪城, Ōsaka-jō) was a pretty sight.

<CaptionedImage
  src={osaka_castle_morning_avif}
  alt="A view of Ōsaka Castle with the glow of early morning sun."
  caption="The early morning sun provided a pretty glow here"
/>

Close to the castle itself, there is a small Japanese garden with a pond in
which you can capture reflections of the castle.

<CaptionedImage

At night, the castle is lit up, providing further photography opportunities.

<CaptionedImage
  src={osaka_castle_night_avif}

  caption="The grounds of the castle also remain open at night"

I also visited Minō Park (mostly written as Minoh Park when romanised locally).
The main walking trail leads to Minō Waterfall.

<CaptionedImage
  src={minoo_waterfall_avif}

It was a nice walk, but it wasn’t quite up there with some of the other places I
visited.

(Of course I did a few other things in Ōsaka too, but nothing I’m going to
bother writing about.)

## Kawaguchiko

I was flying back home from Haneda Airport, so Tokyo was my last stop. I visited
a few exhibitions and then went on a day trip to Lake Kawaguchi (河口湖,
Kawaguchi-ko) not far from Mount Fuji.

I arrived around noon, and Fuji was visible for around two hours.

<CaptionedImage
  src={fuji_near_bridge_avif}
  alt="A view of Mount Fuji and Lake Kawaguchi."
  caption="It was warmer than I expected in the early afternoon"
/>

I walked to the north side of the lake, trying to find a good spot to take a
photo of the mountain (or volcano, if you prefer).

<CaptionedImage
  src={fuji_from_north_avif}
  alt="A view of Mount Fuji and Lake Kawaguchi with some foliage in view in the foreground."
  caption="A bit of foliage in the foreground adds a nice touch"
/>

I’ll be honest, I was a bit disappointed as Fuji didn’t seem any clearer than
what I’d already seen from the Tōkaidō Shinkansen. Though, checking a map
properly, the lake isn’t quite as close to the mountain as I imagined, so
perhaps my expectations were off.

By 2:30pm, cloud had obscured almost all of the mountain. I had a lavender and
Japanese maple ice cream in Ōishi Park and then moved on to the Maple Corridor
where the annual Momiji (autumn leaves) festival had started the day before.
Mostly there were lots of stalls, including food outlets for (in my case) an
early evening meal.

As sunset approached, all the trees in the area lit up. It was pretty, even if
the autumn colours hadn’t quite arrived yet.

<CaptionedImage
  src={maple_corridor_lights_avif}
  alt="The Maple Corridor near the north side of Lake Kawaguchi in the late afternoon with the trees lit up."
  caption="The lights helpfully turned on while I was taking photos (perhaps someone saw me)"
/>

I was knackered after all that walking around the lake, so I had a lazy final
day in Tokyo, getting some shopping done while it rained.

Will I visit Japan a fourth time? I don’t know, but if I did, I’d need to pick
some new areas to visit.
